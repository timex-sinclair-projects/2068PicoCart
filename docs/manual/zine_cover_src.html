User's manual

A cartridge for the 2068's dock slot that holds as many carts as you put on it. Copy cartridge files onto it from your computer like a thumb drive, then choose one from a menu on the 2068's screen.

.dck · .bin · .rom · USB-CRev 0.1 and 0.2, 2026

2068 PicoCart: open hardware (CERN-OHL-P-2.0) and firmware (MIT) from the timex-sinclair-projects group. Design files, firmware and fab files: github.com/timex-sinclair-projects/2068PicoCart.

This manual covers rev 0.1 and rev 0.2 boards with the November 2026 firmware. It was written from the firmware source and tested on a rev 0.1 board in a 2068.

Quick start

  1. Plug the PicoCart into your computer with a USB-C data cable. A drive called PICOCART appears.
  2. Copy a few carts onto it: .dck files, or .bin/.rom images. Eject the drive and unplug.
  3. With the 2068 switched off, push the PicoCart into the dock slot, gold fingers first. The notch in the fingers fits the key in the slot.
  4. Switch the 2068 on. The red light comes on and the cart you copied last starts.
  5. To pick another cart, switch off, hold MENU, and switch on again. Choose with Q and A, start with ENTER.
RememberThe 2068 reads the dock only at power-up. Whenever both lights blink together, a change is waiting: switch the 2068 off and on.

01The cartridge

The PicoCart goes in the 2068's dock slot, under the flap at the front, like any Timex cartridge. Inside is a Raspberry Pi RP2354B microcontroller with 2 MB of flash. It keeps your carts on a small drive, and when the 2068 reads the dock it answers with the cart you chose, as fast as a real ROM.

It holds one 64 KB dock image at a time: all eight 8 KB chunks of the dock bank, each ROM or RAM as the cart file says. So it runs LROS carts at $0000, AROS carts at $8000, and carts that need RAM in the dock.

PartWhat it does
1MENUBrings up the cart menu. The button farther from the USB socket.
2BOOTOnly for firmware updates.
3Red lightOn while the cart is on the 2068's bus.
4White lightFlickers while the 2068 reads the cart.
5USB-CTo your computer, for copying carts. Optional in the 2068.

Rev 0.2 boards have MENU and BOOT printed by the buttons and "rev 0.2" underneath. Rev 0.1 boards say SW1 (MENU) and SW2 (BOOT).

Power off firstSwitch the 2068 off before you put the PicoCart in or take it out. Push it fully home: a contact that doesn't touch shows up as garbled text and carts that crash.

02Loading carts over USB

Plug the PicoCart into your computer with a USB-C data cable, in the 2068 or on its own. A 1.4 MB drive called PICOCART appears, with a README.TXT on it.

  1. Copy a .dck, .bin or .rom file onto the drive.
  2. Eject the drive, or wait a couple of seconds.
  3. Switch the 2068 off and on. It reads the dock only at power-up.

The PicoCart serves the file you copied most recently, and remembers it: the same cart comes up at every power-up until you copy another or choose one from the menu.

If the 2068 is running when you copy, its cart stays put. Both lights blink together while the new cart waits for the next power-up.

Reset isn't enoughA reset (or RANDOMIZE USR 0) restarts the same cart. Only switching off and on brings in a new one.

What the drive holds

  • Only files in the top folder count. Folders, hidden files and names starting with a dot (a Mac's ._ files) are ignored.
  • Up to 48 carts, with names up to 63 characters. 1.4 MB holds 48 carts of 16 KB, or about 40 of 32 KB.
  • Delete the current cart and the PicoCart uses the newest file left.

03The cart menu

To switch between carts on the drive, hold MENU while you switch the 2068 on, until the menu appears. No USB is needed.

It lists the carts in alphabetical order with the current cart highlighted. The first entry, (no cartridge: BASIC), starts the 2068 as if the dock were empty.

KeyDoes
Q 7 ↑Move up
A 6 ↓Move down
ENTER 0Start the highlighted cart

Hold a key to keep moving. The list shows 20 entries and scrolls; names over 30 characters are cut short.

03Starting a cart

Press ENTER and the menu says Starting.... About a second later the 2068 restarts into your cart, as if you had switched on with it in the dock. There's no power cycle. The cart you choose becomes the current cart from then on.

(no cartridge: BASIC) takes the PicoCart off the bus until you switch off: the red light goes out. Next power-up, your current cart is back.

If a file can't be loaded, the 2068 comes back to the menu.

04MENU while a cart runs

Press MENU at any time while a cart runs. The cart carries on, but both lights blink together, and the menu comes up the next time you switch the 2068 on. Press MENU again to cancel. The press is saved in flash at once, so it survives switching off without USB.

05The lights

RedWhiteMeans
onflickersA cart is running and the 2068 is reading it.
onoffOn the bus, but the 2068 isn't reading the dock just now. Normal.
offoffThe 2068 is off, you chose "no cartridge", or the PicoCart is on USB alone.
both blinkWaiting for the next power-up: a new cart, or the menu after a MENU press.

On rev 0.1 boards the firmware dims the white light, which is otherwise far brighter than the red one. Rev 0.2 boards use a larger resistor instead.

06Using it without USB

The PicoCart runs on the 2068's own +5 V and keeps everything in flash: the carts, the current cart and a MENU press. Fill the drive once and leave the cable at your computer:

  1. Plug the PicoCart into your computer on its own.
  2. Copy carts on, eject the drive, unplug.
  3. Put it in the 2068 and switch on. Choose carts with the menu.

On the 2068's power alone it is ready almost two seconds before the 2068 first reads the dock. You can plug USB in and out while the 2068 runs; the cart carries on.

07Cartridge files

.dck

The dock format of Warajevo, EightyOne, ZEsarUX and most cart archives. Each 8 KB chunk is marked ROM, RAM or empty, and the PicoCart sets the dock up the same way. Only the dock bank is used; EXROM and HOME sections are skipped.

.bin and .rom

A raw ROM image, the bytes and nothing else. It loads at $8000 (AROS) if it is 32 KB or smaller, else at $0000. To choose, put a hex address after @ in the name: GAME@0000.BIN. It must end by $FFFF.

LROS carts as .binA 16 KB LROS cart (one that replaces the HOME ROM) must load at $0000: name it like RAIDERS@0000.BIN. Without the address it lands at $8000 and the 2068 won't find it.

Any part of the 64 KB a file doesn't fill reads $FF, as an empty dock would. Other files are ignored, so notes can share the drive.

How the 2068 finds a cart

At power-up the 2068's ROM looks for a cart header at $0000 (LROS), then at $8000 (AROS), and otherwise starts BASIC. The PicoCart answers both from the image you chose, so carts start exactly as from a real cartridge.

08Troubleshooting

ProblemTry this
Garbled menu names; a cart that worked crashes.A dock contact isn't touching. Switch off, clean the gold fingers, push the cart fully home.
The 2068 starts in BASIC.Red light off? You chose "no cartridge": switch off and on. A 16 KB LROS .bin needs @0000.
A new cart doesn't start.Eject the drive, check both lights blink, switch off and on. Reset alone won't do.
No menu.Hold MENU, not BOOT, from before you switch on until the menu appears.
A cart is missing from the menu.Top folder only; .dck, .bin or .rom; name under 64 characters; first 48 carts.
The menu comes back after ENTER.The file couldn't be loaded: damaged, or a .dck with no dock data.
No PICOCART drive.Try another cable: many carry power only. A drive called RP2350 means BOOT was held: unplug and plug in again.
The drive is corrupted.Reformat it as FAT (MS-DOS FAT) and copy your carts back.

Status messages

Over USB the PicoCart is also a serial port. Open it in a terminal program, at any speed, to see lines like 2068 on: serving Profile.dck or new cart waiting: switch the 2068 off and on, and a status line every second.

09Updating the firmware

The firmware comes as romemu.uf2, built from the project's firmware/romemu folder. Updating it leaves the drive alone: your carts and settings stay. Take the PicoCart out of the 2068 first.

With the BOOT button

  1. Hold BOOT and plug the PicoCart into your computer. Let go.
  2. A drive called RP2350 appears.
  3. Copy romemu.uf2 onto it. The PicoCart restarts with the new firmware and PICOCART comes back.

With picotool

With the PicoCart plugged in and running, no button needed:

picotool load -f -x romemu.uf2

-f restarts the cart into its bootloader; -x starts the new firmware.

Rev 0.2 boardsThe firmware dims the white light to 3% to suit rev 0.1. Build rev 0.2 firmware with -DLED2_PERCENT=100; otherwise its white light is just very dim.

11About this manual

Written from the firmware source and tested on a rev 0.1 board in a 2068. The menu screens are from the menu program in ZEsarUX, with the carts from the test board: 73000 Flight Simulator, Profile 2068, Space Raiders, a Hello-world cart and a RAM test.

The PicoCart is open hardware (CERN-OHL-P-2.0) with MIT-licensed firmware. The KiCad design, firmware, JLCPCB fab files and design notes are at github.com/timex-sinclair-projects/2068PicoCart.
