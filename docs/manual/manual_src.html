2068 PicoCart

A cartridge for the 2068's dock slot that holds as many carts as you put on it. Copy cartridge files onto it from your computer like a thumb drive, then choose one from a menu on the 2068's screen.

01The cartridge

The PicoCart goes in the 2068's dock slot, under the flap at the front, like any Timex cartridge. Inside it is a Raspberry Pi RP2354B microcontroller with 2 MB of flash. It stores your carts on a small drive, and when the 2068 reads the dock it answers with the cart you chose, as fast as a real ROM would.

It holds one 64 KB dock image at a time: all eight 8 KB chunks of the dock bank. Each chunk can be ROM or RAM, as the cart file says, so the PicoCart runs ROM carts at $0000 (LROS) and at $8000 (AROS), and carts that need RAM in the dock.

Buttons, lights and the socket

PartWhat it does
1MENU (SW1)Brings up the cart menu. The button farther from the USB socket.

Rev 0.2 boards have MENU and BOOT printed beside the buttons, and "rev 0.2" on the underside. Rev 0.1 boards say SW1 and SW2.

Power off firstSwitch the 2068 off before you put the PicoCart in or take it out, as with any cartridge. Push it fully home: a contact that doesn't touch shows up as garbled text and carts that crash (see Troubleshooting).

02Loading carts over USB

Plug the PicoCart into your computer with a USB-C data cable. A drive called PICOCART appears, 1.4 MB in size, with a README.TXT on it. The cart can be in the 2068 or on its own; USB powers it either way.

  1. Copy a .dck, .bin or .rom file onto the drive.
  2. Eject the drive (or wait a couple of seconds after the copy finishes).
  3. Switch the 2068 off and on. The 2068 reads the dock only when it powers up.

The PicoCart serves the file you copied most recently. It remembers that choice in flash, so the same cart comes up every time you switch on, until you copy another file or choose one from the menu.

If the 2068 is running when you copy, the cart it is running stays put. Both lights blink together to tell you a new cart is waiting; it starts the next time you switch the 2068 off and on.

Reset isn't enoughA 2068 reset (or RANDOMIZE USR 0) restarts the same cart. Only switching the 2068 off and on brings in a new one.

What the drive will hold

  • Only files in the top folder count. Files inside folders are ignored, and so are hidden files and names that start with a dot (the ._ files a Mac leaves behind are harmless).
  • Up to 48 carts. Names can be up to 63 characters; longer names are skipped.
  • 1.4 MB holds 48 carts of 16 KB, or about 40 of 32 KB. A .dck file is 9 bytes bigger than the ROM it holds.

Deleting carts is fine. If you delete the current cart, the PicoCart uses the newest file left on the drive.

On a Mac the drive shows a cartridge icon. Two hidden files make it, .VolumeIcon.icns and ._.; they take about 31 KB, and if you delete them they come back the next time the PicoCart starts.

03The cart menu

To switch between carts already on the drive, hold MENU while you switch the 2068 on, and keep it held until the menu appears. You don't need USB for this.

The menu lists every cart on the drive in alphabetical order, with the current cart highlighted. The first entry, (no cartridge: BASIC), starts the 2068 as if the dock were empty.

KeyDoes
Q 7 ↑Move up.
A 6 ↓Move down.
ENTER 0Start the highlighted cart.

Hold a key to keep moving. The list shows 20 entries and scrolls; names longer than 30 characters are cut short on screen.

Starting a cart

Press ENTER and the menu says Starting.... About a second later the 2068 restarts into the cart you chose, just as if you had switched it on with that cart in the dock. There's no power cycle.

The cart you choose becomes the current cart. It comes up every time you switch on from then on, without the menu.

Choosing (no cartridge: BASIC) takes the PicoCart off the bus until you next switch off: the red light goes out and the 2068 behaves as if the dock were empty. Next time you switch on, your current cart is back.

If a file can't be loaded, the 2068 comes back to the menu.

04MENU while a cart runs

Holding a button while you reach for the power switch is awkward, so you can also press MENU at any time while a cart runs: nothing happens to the running cart, but both lights start blinking together, and the menu comes up the next time you switch the 2068 on.

Changed your mind? Press MENU again before you switch off. The lights stop blinking and the next power-up starts the current cart as usual.

The press is saved in flash at once, so it survives switching off even when the PicoCart has no USB power.

05The lights

RedWhiteMeans
onflickeringA cart is running and the 2068 is reading it. Normal.
onoffOn the bus, but the 2068 isn't reading the dock just now (it is running from its own ROM or RAM). Also normal.
offoffThe 2068 is off, or you chose "no cartridge", or the PicoCart is on USB alone.
both blinking togetherSomething is waiting for the next power-up: a newly copied cart, or the menu after a MENU press. Switch the 2068 off and on.

On rev 0.1 boards the white light is dimmed by the firmware, because its resistor makes it far brighter than the red one. Rev 0.2 boards use a larger resistor instead (see Updating the firmware).

06Using it without USB

The PicoCart doesn't need USB in the 2068. It runs on the 2068's own +5 V, and keeps everything in flash: the carts on the drive, the current cart, and a MENU press. So you can fill the drive once at your computer and leave the cable there.

  1. Plug the PicoCart into your computer on its own. The PICOCART drive appears.
  2. Copy carts onto it, eject the drive and unplug the cable.
  3. Put the PicoCart in the 2068 and switch on. Choose carts with the menu.

From the 2068's power alone, the PicoCart is ready almost two seconds before the 2068 first looks at the dock. You can plug USB in while the 2068 is running (to copy a cart or watch the status messages) and unplug it again; the cart keeps running.

07Cartridge files

TypeWhat it isWhere it goes
.dckThe dock format used by Warajevo, EightyOne, ZEsarUX and most cart archives. Each 8 KB chunk is marked ROM, RAM or empty.Where the file says. RAM chunks, with or without contents, become RAM. Only the dock bank is used; EXROM and HOME sections are skipped.
.bin .romA raw ROM image: the bytes and nothing else.$8000 (AROS) if it is 32 KB or smaller, else $0000. Put an address in the name to choose: GAME@0000.BIN.
LROS carts as .binA 16 KB LROS cart (one that replaces the HOME ROM, such as a game that starts without BASIC) must load at $0000. Name it with @0000, for example RAIDERS@0000.BIN; without it, it lands at $8000 and the 2068 won't find it. A .dck file needs no help.

The address after @ is in hex, with or without a $. The image must fit below $FFFF. Any part of the 64 KB the file doesn't fill reads as $FF, as an empty dock would. Files with other extensions are ignored, so you can keep notes or instructions on the drive alongside the carts.

How the 2068 finds a cart

At power-up the 2068's ROM looks in the dock for a cart header: at $0000 for an LROS cart, then at $8000 for an AROS cart. If it finds neither, it starts BASIC. The PicoCart answers both places from the image you chose, so carts start exactly as they do from a real cartridge.

08Troubleshooting

ProblemTry this
The menu's names are garbled, or a cart that used to work crashes or shows rubbish.A dock contact isn't touching. Switch off, take the PicoCart out, clean the gold fingers if they're dirty, and push it fully home. A single open address line produces exactly this.
The 2068 starts in BASIC as if the dock were empty.Is the red light on? If not, you may have chosen "no cartridge": switch off and on. Check that the file is a cart (section 7); a 16 KB LROS .bin needs @0000 in its name.
A new cart doesn't start.Eject the drive, wait for both lights to blink, then switch the 2068 off and on. Reset alone doesn't change carts.
The menu doesn't come up.Hold MENU (not BOOT) before you switch on, and keep holding until the menu appears. Or press MENU once while a cart runs, check both lights blink, and switch off and on.
A cart is missing from the menu.It must be in the top folder, end in .dck, .bin or .rom, have a name under 64 characters, and be among the first 48 carts.
The menu comes back after ENTER.The file couldn't be loaded: it may be damaged, or a .dck with no ROM or RAM data in the dock bank.
No PICOCART drive on the computer.Try another cable: many USB-C cables carry power only. If a drive called RP2350 appears instead, BOOT was held as you plugged in; unplug and plug in again.
The drive is corrupted or won't mount.Reformat it from your computer as FAT (MS-DOS FAT), then copy your carts back.

Status messages

Over USB the PicoCart is also a serial port. Open it in any terminal program (the speed doesn't matter) to see what it is doing, for example 2068 on: serving Profile.dck, new cart waiting: switch the 2068 off and on, or drive: GAME.BIN is not a cart image I can use. Once a second it prints a status line with the dock voltage and how many times the 2068 has read the cart.

09Updating the firmware

The firmware is the program the PicoCart's microcontroller runs. It comes as a file called romemu.uf2, built from the firmware/romemu folder of the project. Updating it doesn't touch the drive: your carts, the current cart and the settings stay.

Take the PicoCart out of the 2068 first.

With the BOOT button

  1. Hold BOOT and plug the PicoCart into your computer. Let go.
  2. A drive called RP2350 appears (instead of PICOCART).
  3. Copy romemu.uf2 onto it. The PicoCart restarts with the new firmware, and PICOCART comes back.

With picotool

If you have Raspberry Pi's picotool, you don't need the button. With the PicoCart plugged in and running:

picotool load -f -x romemu.uf2

The -f asks the running firmware to restart into the bootloader, and -x starts the new firmware when it's loaded.

Rev 0.2 boardsThe firmware dims the white light to 3% by default, to suit rev 0.1 boards. Rev 0.2 boards have a 3.3 kΩ resistor instead and want full brightness: build their firmware with -DLED2_PERCENT=100. On a rev 0.2 board with rev 0.1 firmware, the white light is just very dim.

11About this manual

The 2068 PicoCart is an open hardware project: the board is licensed under CERN-OHL-P-2.0 and the firmware under the MIT licence. The KiCad design, firmware, fab files for JLCPCB and the design notes are at github.com/timex-sinclair-projects/2068PicoCart.

This manual covers rev 0.1 and rev 0.2 boards with the November 2026 firmware. It was written from the firmware source and from testing on a rev 0.1 board in a 2068: every feature described here was tried on the hardware. The menu screens are from the menu program running in ZEsarUX, with the same carts as the test board: 73000 Flight Simulator, Profile 2068, Space Raiders, a Hello-world test cart and a RAM test.
